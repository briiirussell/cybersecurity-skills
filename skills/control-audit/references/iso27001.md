# ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — ISMS and Annex A reference

For `control-audit`. ISO text is copyrighted and sold by ISO and national bodies. Nothing here quotes it: clause and control titles are paraphrased so you can find the right item, and the requirement text has to come from the organization's licensed copy. Implementation guidance per control is in ISO/IEC 27002:2022.

## Version

- **ISO/IEC 27001:2022**, published October 2022, plus **Amendment 1:2024** (February 2024), which adds climate change to clauses 4.1 and 4.2: decide whether it's a relevant issue, and note that interested parties may have climate-related requirements. Certificates are issued against 27001:2022 including the amendment.
- **Transition from 27001:2013 ended 31 October 2025.** Certificates against the 2013 edition are no longer valid. If an organization still holds or quotes a 2013 certificate, that's the first thing to raise.
- Companion: **ISO/IEC 27002:2022** (guidance and control attributes). Not certifiable on its own.

## How certification works (vs SOC 2)

| | ISO/IEC 27001 | SOC 2 |
|---|---|---|
| What's assessed | The management system (clauses 4–10) and the controls the organization chose (Annex A, via the SoA) | Controls against the Trust Services Criteria |
| Result | Certificate from an accredited certification body, valid 3 years | Attestation report from a CPA firm |
| Cycle | Stage 1 (documentation and readiness) → Stage 2 (implementation and effectiveness) → surveillance audits in years 2 and 3 → recertification | Type 1 once, then Type 2 periods back to back |
| Outcome language | Major / minor nonconformities, opportunities for improvement | Exceptions, qualified or unqualified opinion |
| Period | No fixed observation period, but Stage 2 expects records that the ISMS has run: at least one internal audit and one management review, and risk treatment underway | 3–12 month period for Type 2 |

A major nonconformity (a clause requirement not met at all, or a systemic control failure) blocks certification until it's closed; a minor one needs a corrective action plan. That's audit language. Severity in `findings.json` still reflects exposure: a missing management review is a major nonconformity and a documentation gap, not a High finding.

| 6.3 | Planning changes to the ISMS (new in 2022) | Change records for the ISMS itself |

The repo rarely evidences clauses 4–10. Treat them as evidence requests to the ISMS owner. Gaps here are almost always documentation gaps, not findings.

## Statement of Applicability (SoA)

Required by 6.1.3. For every one of the 93 Annex A controls (and any extra controls the organization added), the SoA states:

- whether it's included or excluded, with the justification for each (inclusion: the risk it treats, or a legal or contractual requirement; exclusion: why it doesn't apply);
- whether it's implemented.

Check: all 93 are listed; every exclusion has a reason that holds up (excluding 8.28 secure coding while shipping software doesn't); every included control traces to a risk or requirement; implementation status matches what the control matrix and evidence show. The SoA is the control inventory for an ISO audit: build Step 2 of `control-audit` from it.

## Annex A (93 controls, 4 themes)

The 2022 edition reorganized the 2013 edition's 114 controls in 14 domains into 93 controls in 4 themes: 11 new, many merged, none dropped in substance. ISO/IEC 27002:2022 also tags each control with five attributes (control type, information security properties, cybersecurity concepts, operational capabilities, security domains), which helps when crosswalking.

Titles below are paraphrased. **New in 2022** is marked ★. The "Repo / hand off" column says where technical evidence usually comes from.

### The 11 new controls at a glance

5.7 threat intelligence · 5.23 cloud services · 5.30 ICT continuity readiness · 7.4 physical monitoring · 8.9 configuration management · 8.10 information deletion · 8.11 data masking · 8.12 data leakage prevention · 8.16 monitoring activities · 8.23 web filtering · 8.28 secure coding.

Organizations that transitioned in a hurry often have these in the SoA as "implemented" with thin evidence. Test them first.

## Fingerprints and ext

- Framework slug: `iso27001`. `ext.framework`: `"iso27001-2022"`.
- Annex A controls: `A.8.32`; clauses: `clause-9.2`.

```
control-audit/iso27001/A.8.32/github/acme/api
control-audit/iso27001/A.5.18/okta-workforce
control-audit/iso27001/A.8.13/aws/123456789012/prod-db
```

Clause gaps (no internal audit, no management review, SoA incomplete) are nearly always documentation gaps, so they rarely get a fingerprint at all.

## Severity, not nonconformity grade

| Gap | Record | Audit language |
|---|---|---|
| Leaver accounts active with production access (A.5.18) | `confirmed`, rated by what the access reaches | Likely major if systemic |
| Production restore never tested and the only backup is in the same account (A.8.13) | `confirmed` if the evidence shows it; Medium–High by data at stake | Minor or major |
| SoA excludes 8.28 secure coding with no reason, and the organization ships software | Documentation gap (the SoA) plus whatever `owasp-audit` finds in the code | Nonconformity against 6.1.3 |
| No management review held yet | Documentation gap | Major at Stage 2 |
| Threat intelligence (5.7) "implemented" but no feed or process | Documentation gap unless a traced exposure follows | Minor |

## Crosswalks

- Map Annex A to NIST CSF 2.0 through `csf-mapping`. NIST publishes informative references between CSF 2.0 and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 in its Online Informative References (OLIR) catalog (verify the current mapping before citing it).
- NIST also publishes a mapping between SP 800-53 Rev. 5 and ISO/IEC 27001 (verify that it's been updated for the 2022 edition).
- SOC 2 ↔ ISO: the AICPA has published a mapping between the Trust Services Criteria and ISO/IEC 27001 (verify the edition it covers). Evidence collected for one is usually reusable for the other; the gaps are the ISMS clauses (no SOC 2 equivalent for management review or the SoA) and SOC 2's system description.
